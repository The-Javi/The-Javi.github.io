N.E.S.T. — VTOL Long-Endurance Surveillance UAS

Senior Design · IPT Lead, Mass Properties · 2026–27

Overview

N.E.S.T. (Next-gen Endurance Surveillance Technology) is my senior design team's answer to a customer brief for a wildfire-spotting aircraft: vertical takeoff and landing, a 3 lb sensor payload, three hours on station, 30 lb maximum takeoff weight, and a $10,000 budget. The program runs like an industry one — a requirements database traced to the customer's documents, and a sequence of formal design reviews before anything gets built.

I'm the Integrated Product Team lead for mass properties, and I lead the composite design of the wing.

The Aircraft

After screening four VTOL concepts — a conventional quadplane, a tandem-wing tiltrotor, a lifting flying wing, and a tailsitter — the team selected the tailsitter flying wing on a weighted trade of empty weight, manufacturing complexity, search-segment L/D, power loading, and actuation complexity. It takes off and lands on its tail, transitions to wing-borne flight, and has the fewest control surfaces and actuators of the candidates.

  • NACA 4415 wing, 7.95 ft span, 10° leading-edge sweep, 0.6 taper ratio
  • Series-hybrid propulsion: a gasoline generator charges the battery that drives two electric motors, giving endurance a battery alone can't
  • Estimated L/D around 18 maximum and 13 in the search segment
  • 3–6 hour search mission at 300 ft AGL with a downward-looking sensor

Mass Properties

Mass properties is a top-down job. The customer's 30 lb limit gets allocated to every subsystem — airframe, propulsion, batteries and fuel, avionics, payload — and each subsystem lead designs to their budget. I track the rollup and the center of gravity as the design matures, because on a tailsitter flying wing with no horizontal tail, CG position is the static margin.

Between the sized baseline (AV BL) and the point-of-departure configuration (AV POD), the vehicle grew as real components replaced estimates:

AV BLAV PODChange
Takeoff gross weight25.59 lb29.74 lb+16%
Useful load13.78 lb15.93 lb+13%
Empty weight11.82 lb13.81 lb+17%
Center of gravity (FS)13.20 in13.11 in−0.1%

Sixteen percent weight growth with the CG held to a tenth of a percent is the number I'm proudest of on this project. The bottoms-up weight sits at 27.64 lb with margin — an 11% design margin fraction against the 30 lb limit, with CG at 44–46% of mean aerodynamic chord at both design gross weight and bottoms-up weight.

The tool behind it is a spreadsheet sizing calculator that links the mass budget to wing loading, so a change in one subsystem's mass immediately shows up as a change in required wing area, stall speed, and the weight of the wing itself. It let us iterate the whole aircraft in minutes rather than re-running the sizing by hand.

Composite Wing

An 8 ft wing is a big part to hand-lay accurately. I'm leading the composite design and tooling to minimize both weight and tolerance stackup, bringing laminate theory, FEA, and three years of Longhorn Racing layups to the question of how to build a wing that comes out of the mold the same shape the aero team analyzed.

Requirements & Reviews

Early in the program I built a source-cited compliance matrix mapping 46 requirements back to the customer's Statement of Work, Capability Development Document, and design and mission specifications, with KPP/KSA scoring. That traceability surfaced eight places where the customer's own documents conflicted — including a threshold-versus-objective mismatch on VTOL thrust margin — which we raised with the customer rather than guessing.

Reviews completed so far: System Requirements Review, Test Readiness Review, Test Results Review, and Conceptual Design Review. Preliminary Design Review is next; Critical Design Review comes before manufacturing in the spring.
